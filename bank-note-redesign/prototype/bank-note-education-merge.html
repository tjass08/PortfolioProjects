Top story

The Fed is redrawing its supervision map

Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman announced plans to organize bank supervision into five regions drawn along state lines, replacing the 12 Reserve Bank districts.

Why it matters: If the Fed supervises your bank or holding company, your examiners aren’t changing. The chain of command above them is. Each region gets one leader responsible for supervision, which the Fed says will simplify decisions and improve coordination with other regulators.

  • Examiners stay in their current Reserve Bank offices and keep the banks they supervise now.
  • Bowman announced the plan at the Community Banking Research Conference in St. Louis.
Read Bowman’s speech
Policy and industry

34 states shut down a crypto kiosk operator

State regulators, including the Iowa Division of Banking, reached a $2.5 million settlement with Coinme Inc. over Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money laundering violations. Coinme must end its kiosk operations by Jan. 1, 2027.

Why it matters: Crypto kiosks are a common tool in the scams your front line works to stop, and Iowa’s regulator was at the table. Read the settlement

Iowa’s economic outlook stays positive

The Iowa Business Council’s third-quarter survey of the state’s 22 largest employers rose slightly to 64.91. Anything above 50 is positive.

Why it matters: Capital spending posted the biggest gain, a good sign for commercial loan demand. Workforce challenges remain a top concern. See the survey

Section name ideas

Credit Hours (recommended)
Two meanings bankers get instantly: credit is their business, and credit hours are what training earns. Short enough for a label and a subject line (“Credit Hours: 8 webinars next week”).
Compound Interest
Learning builds on itself the way interest does. Warm, a little clever.
Capital Gains
Carries the Bulletin’s old “Opportunities for growth” tagline. Leans toward professional development.
The Learning Ledger
Every training on the books. Literal, easy to explain to Education.
Rate Sheet
Plays on the sheet every banker knows. Fits the scannable list format best.

Use the buttons above the email to see each name in place.

Education’s requirements, met

  • Every webinar listed and linked. All 8 from the Bulletin appear, each with its own registration link.
  • Seminars can be highlighted. Four in-person events are shown, with “Last call” on the one happening this week, plus a link to the full calendar.
  • Training tool of the week carries over from the Bulletin as one line.

What Education gets in the upgrade

  • Its own brand inside Bank Note. A standing banner in the same spot every week, so bankers learn where training lives.
  • A line in “In this issue.” Training gets a teaser above the fold every week.
  • Two shots at every webinar. List each webinar from the issue before its date through the date itself. With a weekly send, most webinars appear twice.
  • Webinar-level click data. Tag each link with utm_content=webinar-[short-name] and Education can see clicks and registrations per webinar.
  • Mobile-friendly listings. One column grouped by day replaces the Bulletin’s two columns, which stack in a confusing order on phones.

The length trade-off

The issue grows from about 750 to about 950 words. About 210 of those are in the education section, mostly listings that bankers scan rather than read. To stay near the 900-word target, Coming up was folded in: Education events moved here, and outside deadlines moved to “Mark your calendar” under Policy and industry.

Confirm with Education

  • Does “every webinar” mean every webinar in the next two weeks, or everything on the calendar? This mockup lists every webinar until the next issue goes out.
  • Who sends the weekly list, and by what day? Suggest Tuesday noon for a Thursday send.
  • Pick the name together. Letting Education choose it helps them own the section.
