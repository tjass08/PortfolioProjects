# Research findings

As of Oct. 12, 2026. Sources are linked inline and collected in `sources.md`.

## Bottom line

Bank Note should be built for a banker who gives it under a minute and skims most of it. Every structural choice should help that reader find the two or three things that matter to them, then choose whether to go deeper.

- **Time is the constraint.** People look at a typical email for about 9 seconds, and newsletter readers average under a minute per issue. The first screen does most of the work.
- **Skimming is the default, not the failure.** Most newsletters get skimmed rather than read. Structure (labels, bold lead-ins, short blocks) is what makes skimming productive.
- **Selection beats compression.** Axios-style issues stay short by picking fewer, better items, not by squeezing long articles into less space.
- **Measure clicks, not opens.** Apple’s privacy features inflate open rates, so clicks per story and click-to-open rate are the honest readouts.

Practical target: roughly 900 words or a three-minute read, an “In this issue” scan list at the top, one lead item with a “Why it matters” line and the popular member sections kept as recurring, recognizable blocks.

## Why Smart Brevity works

Smart Brevity has four parts: a tease (headline), a lede (the one new thing), context (“Why it matters”) and choice (a link to go deeper). Axios’ design team built the product around what readers actually do, which is skim ([Society for News Design](https://snd.org/alexis-lloyd-speaks-at-sndnyc-about-effective-design-from-the-ground-up/); [getAbstract](https://journal.getabstract.com/?p=104621)).

## Gaps

- None of these sources studies Iowa bankers specifically. Fill the gap with Bank Note’s own data (device mix, click map by section, open times) and conversations with member bankers.
- Bankers likely read at work in desktop Outlook during business hours. Confirm in the email platform’s reports before designing around it.
- The Nielsen Norman newsletter studies are older; newer Litmus data points the same direction.
